# Format notes

What the files contain, as found by reading real files byte by byte, and the places where the specifications or intuition led somewhere wrong. Each note says how it was confirmed. "LibreOffice" below means comparing against LibreOffice's PDF export of the same file.

## PPTX

A zip of XML parts. Every shape has an absolute position (`<a:xfrm>`, in EMU), so there is no layout to compute; the work is resolving inheritance. Checked against LibreOffice on 34 real decks (see TESTING).

- **Inheritance chain.** A slide uses a layout, the layout a master, the master a theme. A slide placeholder matches the layout placeholder with the same `idx` (or, without one, the same type), and that one the master placeholder of the same kind (title, body, date, footer, slide number; subtitles and content placeholders map to body). Position, geometry, fill, outline, body properties (`<a:bodyPr>` anchor, insets, wrap, autofit) and list styles are each taken from the nearest level that sets them. Most placeholders on real slides store no position at all, and a title placeholder's bottom anchor usually lives on the master, so skipping either level drops or misplaces text.
- **Text styles, nearest first:** the run, the paragraph, the shape's own `<a:lstStyle>`, the layout placeholder's, the master placeholder's, then the master's `titleStyle`, `bodyStyle` or `otherStyle`. Text boxes that aren't placeholders use the presentation's `defaultTextStyle`.
- **Backgrounds** come from the slide, else the layout, else the master: `<p:bgPr>` with a solid, gradient or picture fill, or `<p:bgRef idx>` into the theme's background fill styles (idx 1001 and up). A picture background is resolved against the relationships of the part that declares it.
- **Colors.** `<a:schemeClr>` goes through the master's `<p:clrMap>` (overridable per layout and slide with `<p:clrMapOvr>`), so a dark master that maps `bg1` to `dk1` comes out dark. Modifiers apply in document order: `lumMod`/`lumOff`/`satMod` in HSL (PowerPoint's "darker 25%" theme variants are `lumMod 75000`), `tint`/`shade` mixed toward white or black in linear light, plus `alpha`. `sysClr`, `prstClr`, `scrgbClr` and `hslClr` are read too.
- **Shape styles.** Shapes inserted with PowerPoint's default style name no fill or outline at all; `<p:style>` refers into the theme's format scheme instead (`<a:fillRef idx="3"><a:schemeClr val="accent1"/>` = the third fill style, with `phClr` standing for accent1). An explicit fill or outline in `<p:spPr>` wins, then the placeholder chain's, then the style's. `<a:fontRef>`'s color is the text color of last resort.
- **Text.** Shrink-on-overflow (`<a:normAutofit fontScale="62500" lnSpcReduction="20000">`) stores the scale PowerPoint computed, which is applied as is (the renderer may shrink a little further where substitute fonts run longer). Bullets and numbers are not shown on empty paragraphs. `<a:buAutoNum>` numbers like the `.ppt` path; percent spacing before and after is measured like the `.ppt` path; `<a:fld>` (slide numbers, dates) shows its saved text; hyperlinks without their own color take the theme's `hlink` color and an underline.
- **Geometry.** Preset shapes are drawn from the specification's formulas, honoring adjust values, which are mostly fractions of the shorter side, so an arrow's head depends on the box's aspect ratio. Connectors (`<p:cxnSp>`) are straight, elbow or curved, with arrowheads at either end. `<a:custGeom>` paths (lines, cubic and quadratic Béziers, `arcTo`) are converted to cubic outlines; paths marked `fill="none"` are stroked only.
- **SVG icons.** Office 365 inserts icons as `<asvg:svgBlip>`, often with no raster fallback in `r:embed`. They're drawn by a small SVG player (paths, basic shapes, transforms, `<style>` class rules, which override presentation attributes).
- **Picture formats are told by content.** A `.emf` part can be a WMF without a placeable header; its extent then comes from its own `SetWindowOrg`/`SetWindowExt` records. A PNG can carry "<svg" inside C2PA metadata, so SVG is only recognized by its root element.
- **SmartArt** is stored as data plus layout rules, but PowerPoint also saves the shapes it drew: the data part's `<dsp:dataModelExt relId>` names a drawing part, whose shapes (in the frame's coordinate space, with their own text rectangles in `<dsp:txXfrm>`) are drawn as ordinary shapes.
- **Effects.** A shape's `<a:effectLst>` wins over its placeholder's and over the theme effect style its `<p:style><a:effectRef idx>` names (`<a:effectStyleLst>`, 1-based); an empty `<a:effectLst/>` means no effects. Office 2007-style themes give the default shape style (effectRef 2) a soft shadow, so default shapes there have one. An `<a:outerShdw>` is a blur radius, a distance and a direction in 1/60000 degree clockwise from the right, and a color (usually with an alpha); it is drawn as the shape's silhouette, offset and blurred, under the shape. `<a:highlight>` in run properties is a background color behind the run's text.
- **Charts** are a `<c:chart r:id>` in a graphic frame, pointing at a chart part. The data lives in an embedded workbook, but the chart part caches every label and value it shows (`<c:strCache>`, `<c:numCache>`, points by `idx` up to `ptCount`), so the chart is drawn from the part alone, as ordinary shapes. The layout is not stored (only optional `<c:manualLayout>` fractions), so it is approximated: the value axis runs in about ten steps of 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten up to the largest value plus 5% of the range, as Excel does; bars of one category share the slot left after `gapWidth` (a percentage of a bar's width), overlapping by `overlap`; pie slices run clockwise from twelve o'clock. Series without their own color take the theme's accents in turn. A chart with one series and no `<c:title>` still shows the series name as its title unless `<c:autoTitleDeleted val="1">`. Text that the chart doesn't style is 10 pt, and titles 18 pt bold.
- **Tables** take cell fills and text color and weight from their table style (`ppt/tableStyles.xml`; the header row, banded rows, first/last column flags). PowerPoint's default style is referenced by id without being stored, so it's built in. A table with no `<a:tableStyleId>` has no style at all: tableStyles.xml's `def` is only what PowerPoint gives tables inserted later (five of 34 real decks had such tables, white text on a dark slide that a default style made unreadable). Row heights (`<a:tr h>`) are minimums; PowerPoint grows a row to fit its cells' text, and the frame's saved height can be smaller than the rows add up to.
- **`mc:AlternateContent`** (equations, newer content) is drawn from its `Fallback` branch, usually a picture.
- **Hidden slides** (`<p:sld show="0">`) are skipped.
- **Not read.** Charts (LibreOffice draws them from the cached data; nothing here does yet), shadows, glow, soft edges, 3-D rotation and bevels, text highlight, strikethrough, `phClr` outside style references, preset shapes outside the list (drawn as rectangles or ellipses).

## PPT (PowerPoint 97–2003)

An OLE2 compound file. The streams that matter are `PowerPoint Document` (records), `Current User` (where the newest edit is) and `Pictures` (embedded images). Records follow MS-PPT for the document structure and MS-ODRAW ("Escher") for shapes. Coordinates are master units, 1/576 inch, which is 1587.5 EMU; a 16:9 slide stores 7680 × 4320.

### Structure

- **Incremental saves.** Each save appends a `UserEditAtom` with its own `PersistDirectoryAtom` and a link to the previous one. Start from `CurrentUserAtom.offsetToCurrentEdit` and keep the first offset seen for each persist id; that is the current version of each object.
- **Slide list.** `SlideListWithText` instance 0 lists slides in order, instance 1 masters, instance 2 notes. Using any other instance draws masters or notes pages as slides.
- **Masters are found by slide id, not persist id.** `SlideAtom.masterIdRef` matches the `slideId` field of a `SlidePersistAtom` in the master list. Looking it up in the persist directory finds the wrong object.
- **What a slide inherits.** `SlideAtom` flags say whether the slide follows its master's objects (logos, bars, footers), color scheme and background. A master can be a title master that in turn follows a main master; text styles always come from the main master's `TextMasterStyleAtom`s.
- **The drawing group is not where its name suggests.** `DggContainer` (default shape properties and the picture store) is inside record `0x040B` (DrawingGroup), not a direct child of the document. The `Environment` container, `0x03F2`, holds the font list and the document's default text formatting.
- **Hidden slides** have bit 2 set in `SSSlideInfoAtom` at offset 10. They are skipped, as PowerPoint's and LibreOffice's PDF export do.

### Shapes

- **`OfficeArtClientAnchor` is (top, left, right, bottom)**, not the (left, top, right, bottom) of a Win32 `RECT`. With the wrong order every shape on a slide lands on the same y. Found by converting a `.ppt` back to `.pptx` and lining up coordinates.
- **Shapes in groups** use a `ChildAnchor` in the group's own coordinate space, given by the group's `FSPGR` rectangle, which maps onto the group's anchor.
- **Text placement inside a shape** depends on the vertical anchor (FOPT property `0x87`). Ignoring it stacks title and subtitle text at the top of their shared box.
- **Default fill and line.** A shape that doesn't set fill or line takes them from the drawing group's default FOPT. Placeholders and text boxes don't: their unset values mean "none", which is what they inherit from the master's placeholder.
- **Complex FOPT properties** (vertex and segment arrays of freeforms) are stored after all the fixed 6-byte entries, in the same order. Some writers (LibreOffice) give an array's length without its own 6-byte header: when `nElems × cbElem` equals the declared length, read 6 more bytes.
- **Gradients.** Linear fills go from `fillColor` to `fillBackColor` in screen direction `90 − fillAngle`. `fillFocus = -50` means axial: `fillColor` at both ends, the back color in the middle. A slide background gradient is stored in the opposite direction. Only calibrated against LibreOffice, since no PowerPoint-made gradient sample was available.

### Colors

- A slide or master has eight scheme colors (background, text, shadow, title, fills, accent, two hyperlink accents); the active set is the `ColorSchemeAtom` with instance 1.
- Shape colors (`OfficeArtCOLORREF`) are RGB, or a scheme index when flag `0x08` is set in the high byte.
- Text colors (`ColorIndexStruct`) are RGB when the last byte is `0xFE`, a scheme index when it is below 8.

### Text

- **Formatting runs.** `StyleTextPropAtom` holds paragraph runs then character runs, each counting one character more than the text atom stores (the implied final paragraph mark).
- **Extension mask bits carry no data here.** In `TextCFException`, the `pp10ext`, `newEATypeface`, `csTypeface` and `pp11ext` mask bits announce fields of `TextCFException10`, which lives in extension records; nothing for them is inline (Apache POI reads them the same way). An older version of this parser gave up on the whole atom when it saw them, so most real files came out as plain 18 pt black text.
- **Master text styles.** A placeholder stores only what differs from the master's `TextMasterStyleAtom` for its text type (title, body, other, center title, …) and indent level. Without them titles lose their size, color and alignment, and bullets disappear.
- **Automatic numbering is in a PowerPoint 2000 extension.** The shape's `ClientData` holds `ProgTags` → a `ProgBinaryTag` named `___PPT9` → `BinaryTagData` → `StyleTextProp9Atom`, an array of {`TextPFException9`, `TextCFException9`, `TextSIException`}. A paragraph's entry is picked by `pp9rt`, bits 10–13 of `fontStyle` in the character run at the paragraph's start. Numbers continue per indent level and restart when a non-numbered paragraph at the same or a shallower level comes between.
- **Bullets are characters in symbol fonts**: Monotype Sorts `n` is ■, `l` is ●; Webdings `4` is ▸; Wingdings has its own table. They're mapped to the Unicode shapes they draw. Text typed in a symbol font, or stored as private-use code points U+F0xx, is translated the same way, using the run's symbol font (`symbolFontRef`) for U+F0xx characters.
- **Tabs.** `TextRulerAtom` gives tab stops, the default tab width and per-level margins. The hanging-indent position itself acts as the first tab stop, as in PowerPoint and Word; without that, columns of tab-separated text shift by one stop.
- **Shrink on overflow** has no on/off flag in the 97–2003 records. The renderer applies it to non-title placeholders, only when the text really overflows (more than 1.5 px, so rounding doesn't count), down to 50%, and ignores trailing empty paragraphs (decks often leave blank lines where a picture goes). These rules separate the slides LibreOffice shrinks from the ones it doesn't on a 75-slide lecture deck. They only work because font metrics match (see Rendering).

### Pictures

- A picture shape's `pib` property is a 1-based index into the drawing group's blip store; each `FBSE` entry's `foDelay` is an offset into the `Pictures` stream.
- Raster blips start with one or two 16-byte GUIDs and sometimes a tag byte. Scanning for the format's magic bytes is simpler than decoding which of those are present.
- Metafile blips (EMF, WMF) have a 34-byte header and are usually zlib-compressed. A WMF blip has no placeable header of its own; one is built from the blip header's `rcBounds`.
- DIB blips are bitmaps without a file header; prefixing a BMP header makes them decodable. TIFF is converted to BMP (uncompressed, LZW, PackBits). PICT is only read when it wraps a JPEG or PNG.

## HWPX

A zip of XML (OWPML). `Contents/content.hpf` lists the parts and their order; `Contents/header.xml` defines fonts, character and paragraph properties and border fills; each `Contents/sectionN.xml` holds the text.

- **Hangul saves its own layout.** Every paragraph has `<hp:linesegarray>`: for each line, the character offset where it starts (`textpos`) and its position and size (`vertpos`, `horzpos`, `horzsize`, `textheight`, `baseline`). A new page shows up as `vertpos` going back up. Checked on a real three-page document: the paragraph LibreOffice puts at the top of page 3 is the one whose first line resets to 0.
- **Positions are relative to the body area**, which starts at the left margin plus gutter and below the top margin plus the header area (`<hp:pagePr><hp:margin>` in the first paragraph's `<hp:secPr>`). Confirmed against LibreOffice's text positions.
- **Use the paragraph's own line array.** A paragraph holding a table also contains the line arrays of the table's cell paragraphs. Searching recursively finds one of those first and puts the table at that cell line's position.
- **Table cell sizes are measured, not trusted.** `<hp:cellSz>` is a declared minimum that real content often outgrows; one sample declared 282 HWPUNIT (under half a line) for a six-line answer. Cell sizes come from the extent of their content's line positions instead. Cell content is offset by `<hp:cellMargin>`, and `vertAlign` on the cell's `<hp:subList>` moves it down within a taller cell.
- **Objects outside the text.** A table, picture or drawing object whose `<hp:pos treatAsChar="0">` floats is placed by `vertRelTo`/`horzRelTo` (PAPER, PAGE, PARA or COLUMN) and the offsets, the same way as in HWP below. `<hp:sz>` can be relative: `widthRelTo="PARA"` with `width="10000"` means 100% of the body width.
- **Drawing objects** (`<hp:rect>`, `<hp:ellipse>`, `<hp:line>`, `<hp:polygon>`, `<hp:container>` for groups) are drawn in their original size (`<hp:orgSz>`) mapped through `<hp:renderingInfo>`: a translation, then a scale and a rotation matrix per group level, each `e1..e6` (x' = e1 x + e2 y + e3, y' = e4 x + e5 y + e6). A group's members go through the group's matrices too. `<hp:lineShape style="NONE">` means no outline; the fill is `<hc:winBrush faceColor>`. A text box is `<hp:drawText>`: `<hp:textMargin>` and a `<hp:subList>` of paragraphs.
- **Picture crop.** `<hp:imgClip>` is the visible part within the image's own size, `<hp:imgDim>`, not within `<hp:imgRect>`: a real photo had an `imgRect` of 600,000 and an `imgClip` right edge of 300,000 that kept the whole image, because `imgDim` was 300,000.
- **Headers and footers** are `<hp:header>`/`<hp:footer>` controls with a `<hp:subList>` of paragraphs (`vertAlign` places them in their area) and `applyPageType` BOTH, ODD or EVEN. They take effect from the page their paragraph is on, replacing earlier ones: a template had four footers, the first a placeholder replaced by the next. The page number position is `<hp:pageNum pos sideChar>`, and `<hp:autoNum numType="PAGE">` in a run is the page number field. `<hp:pageHiding hideHeader hideFooter hidePageNum>` hides those on its own page only (a cover page), and `<hp:newNum numType="PAGE" num>` starts page numbering over from its page.
- **Tabs** are `<hp:tab width leader type/>` elements inside `<hp:t>`, which a plain text read drops. Each saves the width the tab took, so right and center tab stops come out where Hangul put them without reading the tab definitions. A tab counts as one character in line offsets. `<hp:nbSpace/>`, `<hp:fwSpace/>` and `<hp:hyphen/>` are a space, a space and a hyphen.
- **Paragraph numbers and bullets** are not stored as text. A paragraph shape's `<hh:heading type idRef level>` names a numbering (`<hh:numbering>`: per level a `<hh:paraHead>` whose text is the format, "^1." or "(^5)", with `numFormat` and `start`), a bullet (`<hh:bullet char>`, often a Wingdings private-use code) or, for OUTLINE, the numbering the section's `outlineShapeIDRef` names. Numbers are counted through the document: a paragraph advances its level and restarts the deeper ones. The saved lines leave no room for the number; Hangul draws it at the start of the first line and the text `textOffset` (percent of the text size) after it.
- **Not handled.** Footnotes. Row and column spans are approximated.

## HWP 5.0

An OLE2 compound file: `FileHeader` (the signature "HWP Document File", version, and flags: bit 0 compressed, bit 1 password, bit 2 distribution document), `DocInfo` and `BodyText/Section0..N`, raw-deflated when compressed (deflate without a zlib header), and pictures in `BinData/BIN%04X.ext`. Specification: Hancom's "한글 문서 파일 형식 5.0". Password-protected and distribution documents are encrypted and are refused.

- **Records.** Each record starts with a 32-bit header: tag (10 bits), level (10 bits) and size (12 bits; 0xFFF means a 32-bit size follows). Levels make a tree: a paragraph's text, character runs, line segments and controls are one level below it, a table's cells and their paragraphs hang off the table's control header.
- **The same saved layout as HWPX.** `PARA_LINE_SEG` holds 36 bytes per line: start offset, y, height, text height, baseline, spacing, x, width and flags, in the same units and relative to the same body area.
- **Text positions count code units, controls included.** In `PARA_TEXT`, characters below 32 are controls. Line break, paragraph end, hyphen and the special spaces take one code unit; every other control takes eight (the code, six of data, the code again), tab included. The extended ones (section and column definitions, tables and drawing objects, headers, footers, footnotes, page number position and others) each have a `CTRL_HEADER` child, in the order they appear in the text.
- **DocInfo** lists fonts per script (as many of each as `ID_MAPPINGS` says), then `BORDER_FILL`, `CHAR_SHAPE` and `PARA_SHAPE` records that paragraphs and cells refer to by index (border fill ids are 1-based). A character shape is 7 face ids, 7 width ratios (장평), 7 spacings (자간), 7 relative sizes and 7 offsets, one per script with Hangul first, then the size in 1/100 pt at byte 42, attributes at 46 (italic, bold, underline, strikethrough, super/subscript) and the text color at 52. Colors are COLORREF, 0x00BBGGRR; a nonzero top byte means no color.
- **Border fills** are four sides of (type, width index, color), a diagonal, and a fill whose type bits are solid color, picture and gradient. A gradient gives a type byte, angle, center, blur, a color count and the colors; it is drawn as their average.
- **Pages.** A page starts where a line goes back up, and also where a paragraph's break-type byte (offset 11 of `PARA_HEADER`) has the page-break bit: several real documents start consecutive pages at y = 0, so a position alone can't tell them apart. Two lines starting at the same height can't be on the same page either.
- **Paragraph numbers and bullets** work as in HWPX: `PARA_SHAPE` attribute bits 23-24 give the heading kind (outline, number, bullet), 25-27 the level, and byte 30 the 1-based numbering or bullet id; the section definition's numbering id for outlines is at byte 18. `NUMBERING` holds seven paragraph heads (attributes with the number shape in bits 5-8, width adjustment, distance, character shape), each followed by its format string, then a start number and each level's start. `BULLET` is a paragraph head and the character.
- **Tabs** are eight-unit controls whose data starts with the width the tab took (u32, HWPUNIT), then its leader and type; drawn as that much space.
- **Floating objects** are placed by their control header's attributes: reference (paper, page, paragraph), offsets, and text wrapping. Saved offsets are not always current: an object that takes its own lines and moves with the text was found 17,000 HWPUNIT above its paragraph while the next paragraph started exactly below the object placed at the paragraph, so such an object is kept at or below its anchor. An object that runs past the bottom of its page moves to the next page when that page's first line starts below where it would go, and stays otherwise; Hangul's saved positions show which.
- **Tables run across pages** in both HWPX and HWP: rows that don't fit continue at the top of the next page, where Hangul's saved positions already leave room for them. A row taller than a page is cut between its cells' lines and continues on the next page. A .hwp cell's declared size is what it had while its row still fit on a page: a cell holding several pages of text (one answer sheet was a single 3-row table) declares about one page, so a row whose content runs well past its declared height takes the content's. Only lines that follow on from each other count: some cells keep a stray line position far below their content.
- **Cell margins of 0xFFFF** (in a cell's `LIST_HEADER`) mean the table's inner margins (`TABLE` bytes 10-17: left, right, top, bottom). Read as numbers, they pushed the cell's text 65535 HWPUNIT down, out of sight.
- **Equations** are `eqed` controls with an `EQEDIT` record: attributes, the script (u16 length, UTF-16), the base font size (1000 = 10 pt), the color, the baseline (percent of the object's height from the top), then a version string and the font name. HWPX keeps the same in `<hp:equation baseUnit baseLine textColor>` with `<hp:script>`. The script is Hangul's EQN-like language: words separated by spaces that take no room, `~` a space and `` ` `` a quarter of one, braces grouping, `over`, `sqrt`/`root … of`, `^`/`_` (and `SUP`/`SUB`/`LSUB`), `sum`/`prod`/`int`/`lim` with `from`/`to`, `LEFT ( … RIGHT )`, `matrix`/`pmatrix`/`bmatrix`/`dmatrix`/`cases`/`pile`, accents (`hat`, `bar`, `vec`, …), `rm`/`it`/`bold`, `"quoted"` text, and named symbols (`TIMES`, `APPROX`, `LEQ`, `==`, `rarrow`, …). Structural keywords ignore case; Greek names don't (`sigma` σ, `SIGMA` Σ). Only the script and the box are stored, so the formula is typeset again and fitted to the box on its baseline.
- **Objects in a line of text.** An object treated as a character in a line that also has text is drawn in the line, taking its saved width, with an equation's baseline (else the object's bottom) on the line's baseline. Drawn on its own, the text after it would start over at the line's left edge.
- **Cells from other writers.** Some files (made by converters, judging by their version) save every line of a cell paragraph at the same height, or one line for a whole paragraph at the page's width. Lines in a cell are stacked when they don't go down, clamped to the cell's width, and wrapped by the renderer when a line is far wider than its column.
- **Objects pushed below.** A paragraph's own top isn't saved, only its lines. At the top of a header, footer or text box the paragraph starts at the list's top, so an object there that takes its own lines and starts within the first line of text has pushed that line below itself, and ends where the line starts (a band drawn above a footer's text). Elsewhere the same rule moved pictures over earlier paragraphs, so it isn't applied.
- **Headers, footers and page numbers** are the `head`, `foot` and `pgnp` controls. Header and footer attributes bits 0-1 say which pages (both, even, odd); like in HWPX they take effect from the page their paragraph is on. A page number position's attributes hold the position in bits 8-11, followed by the user, prefix, suffix and side characters (u16 each, the side one at byte 14). An `atno` control of type 0 is the page number field. `pghd` hides parts on its own page (attribute bits 0 header, 1 footer, 5 page number; 14 of 54 real files use it, mostly on a cover page), and `nwno` starts numbering over (number type in attribute bits 0-3, 0 for pages, then the number as u16). A paragraph holding only such controls has nothing to draw; its page comes from where its first line is saved. When one paragraph holds two headers (or footers) for the same pages, the first counts: a template had a second, empty footer after its table, and LibreOffice too shows the first (a logo).
- **Drawing objects** (`gso` controls) hold a `SHAPE_COMPONENT` tree. A top-level component repeats its control id; after the id(s) come offsets, group level and version, original and current size (bytes 12-27 from there), flags, rotation and its center, then the rendering matrices: a count, a translation matrix and count pairs of scale and rotation matrices, each six doubles. Then the outline (color, width, attributes with the line type in bits 0-5, 0 = none, an outline style byte) and the fill. A group (`$con`) has its members as child components; a shape's points are in a child record (`SHAPE_COMPONENT_LINE`: start and end; `_RECTANGLE`: a corner curvature byte and four corners; `_POLYGON`: a count and points). A text box is a `LIST_HEADER` (flags with the vertical alignment in bits 5-6, then left, right, top and bottom margins) and paragraphs among the component's children. A straight line has a zero height.
- **Picture crop.** `SHAPE_COMPONENT_PICTURE` gives the image's corners (bytes 12-43) and the visible part as left, top, right, bottom (44-59), within the image's own size, which newer files save near the end of the record (width and height in the last 9 bytes). Taking the corners as that size cut real pictures to a third.
- **Not handled.** Footnotes, charts, multiple columns, and the older HWPML XML format some `.hwp` files are saved in.

## Rendering

These are not stored in the files; they are where Android's text defaults differ from PowerPoint's and had to be measured.

- **Fonts.** Line breaks only match when glyph widths match. Liberation Sans, Serif and Mono have the metrics of Arial/Helvetica, Times New Roman and Courier New. With Android's Roboto, lines came out about 5% shorter, so text wrapped elsewhere and looked smaller.
- **Exact sizes and widths.** Font sizes keep their fractions (Android's `AbsoluteSizeSpan` truncates to whole pixels), and text is measured unhinted with subpixel positioning. Hinted widths round each glyph to a whole pixel, which made lines about 4% longer.
- **Line height.** Every line, including a paragraph's first, is `largest font size on the line × 1.2 × line spacing %`, carrying fractions into the next line. A subscript that drops below the font's descent makes its line taller. Space before and after a paragraph given in percent is `size × 1.25 × %`. Both were measured from text baselines in LibreOffice's PDF output (14 pt at 90% → 15.12 pt between baselines). `StaticLayout` instead uses the font's own height (1.117 em) and applies the multiplier only between lines.
- **Bullets as shapes.** ●, ■, ◆ and ► are drawn as shapes rather than glyphs, because a device's fallback font draws ● much smaller than Office does. Number bullets use the paragraph's font and weight.
- **Large pictures.** A 2000 px diagram drawn into a 260 px box is decoded with `inSampleSize` and then halved step by step. One bilinear downscale made thin lines and small text disappear.
- **Text inside metafiles.** Symbol-font characters are translated, per-character spacing arrays (`dx`) are honored, and a positive font height is a cell height (divided by 1.15 for the em size). Equations stored as WMF (τ, α, ≤) only came out right with all three.
- **EMF and WMF playback** covers paths, polygons, polylines, Béziers, rectangles, ellipses, pens, brushes, fonts, text, embedded DIBs and the window, viewport and world transforms. Clipping, raster operations, hatch patterns and EMF+ records are ignored.
